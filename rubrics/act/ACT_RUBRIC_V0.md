# TheraSim — ACT Session-Level Evaluator Rubric

**Rubric ID:** ACT_RUBRIC_V0
**Version:** 0.2
**Status:** Experimental / Not Validated
**Evaluation Scope:** Single Session
**Last Updated:** 2026-11-14

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This rubric evaluates observable therapist behavior during one completed psychotherapy simulation session.

It is designed for formative educational feedback within the TheraSim ACT simulation environment.

The rubric assesses:

- Therapeutic relationship.
- Functional assessment.
- Psychological workability.
- Acceptance and willingness.
- Cognitive defusion.
- Present-moment awareness.
- Values clarification.
- Committed action.
- Clinical responsiveness.
- Safety and professional boundaries.

This rubric is not a validated competency instrument, clinical certification system, diagnostic tool, or replacement for professional supervision.

### 1.1 Evaluation Scope

This rubric applies exclusively to one session.

It must not be used to assess:

- Continuity across multiple sessions.
- Longitudinal therapeutic formulation.
- Changes in therapist competence over time.
- Long-term treatment outcomes.

These dimensions require a separate longitudinal evaluation rubric.

### 1.2 Evaluation Evidence

The evaluator must assess the therapist using:

1. The finalized session transcript.
2. Therapist-visible session context.
3. The applicable rubric version.
4. Explicit evaluation instructions.

The evaluator must not access hidden case formulation, undisclosed personal history, private simulation state, or internal Patient Agent instructions.

---

## 2. Scoring Anchors

Each competency is rated using the following scale.

### 0 — Absent or Potentially Counterproductive

The relevant behavior is absent despite a reasonable opportunity, or the therapist's response may undermine the therapeutic process.

### 1 — Emerging

The skill is partially demonstrated but is inconsistent, poorly timed, or insufficiently collaborative.

### 2 — Adequate

The skill is demonstrated in a relevant and reasonably context-sensitive manner.

### 3 — Strong

The skill is applied flexibly, collaboratively, and with clear attention to the client's context.

### NE — Not Enough Evidence

The transcript does not provide sufficient evidence to make a defensible judgment.

**NE must never be converted into zero.**

### 2.1 Rating Principles

1. Ratings must reflect observable therapist behavior.
2. Technical terminology alone does not demonstrate competency.
3. A therapist intervention must be evaluated in its conversational context.
4. The absence of an opportunity to demonstrate a competency must not automatically be rated zero.
5. A patient's positive response does not automatically establish therapist competency.
6. A patient's negative or uncertain response does not automatically establish poor therapist performance.
7. Ratings must not be based on hidden information unavailable to the therapist.
8. The evaluator must distinguish between an omitted intervention and an intervention that was not clinically or conversationally appropriate at that point.

## 3. Evaluation Dimensions

The rubric contains ten competency dimensions.

### A. Therapeutic Relationship

**Competency ID:** therapeutic_relationship

Assess:

- Empathy.
- Validation.
- Collaboration.
- Respect for client autonomy.
- Sensitivity to hesitation.
- Nonjudgmental communication.
- Responsiveness to interpersonal feedback.

The evaluator should examine whether the therapist establishes a collaborative and respectful interaction.

Do not equate excessive reassurance or agreement with therapeutic empathy.

### B. Functional Assessment

**Competency ID:** functional_assessment

Assess:

- Identification of relevant triggers.
- Exploration of private experiences.
- Identification of avoidance behavior.
- Examination of immediate consequences.
- Examination of longer-term consequences.
- Exploration of contextual and maintaining factors.

The evaluator should assess whether the therapist explores the function of the client's behavior rather than relying exclusively on symptom descriptions.

Do not require discovery of hidden information that the client has not disclosed.

### C. Psychological Workability

**Competency ID:** psychological_workability

Assess whether the therapist explores whether the client's current strategies move the client toward or away from meaningful life activities.

The evaluator should consider:

- Short-term usefulness of existing coping strategies.
- Longer-term costs of avoidance.
- Relationship between behavior and meaningful activities.
- Exploration of alternative responses.
- Collaborative examination of workability.

The therapist should not be penalized for allowing the client to explore the question gradually.

### D. Acceptance and Willingness

**Competency ID:** acceptance_willingness

Assess whether the therapist appropriately explores openness to difficult internal experiences instead of focusing exclusively on eliminating or controlling them.

The evaluator should consider:

- Exploration of experiential avoidance.
- Understanding of the client's struggle with internal experiences.
- Collaborative exploration of willingness.
- Avoidance of coercive acceptance instructions.
- Sensitivity to the client's readiness.

Acceptance must not be confused with passive resignation or forced exposure.

### E. Cognitive Defusion

**Competency ID:** cognitive_defusion

Assess whether the therapist helps the client change their relationship with thoughts rather than relying exclusively on disputing thought content.

The evaluator should consider:

- Recognition of cognitive fusion when supported by evidence.
- Appropriate defusion exercises.
- Clear and accessible explanations.
- Collaborative application.
- Responsiveness to the client's actual experience.

Mentioning cognitive defusion or naming an ACT exercise does not automatically justify a positive rating.

### F. Present-Moment Awareness

**Competency ID:** present_moment_awareness

Assess appropriate attention to ongoing thoughts, feelings, sensations, and experiences.

The evaluator should consider:

- Appropriate experiential exercises.
- Attention to current experience.
- Clear guidance.
- Responsiveness to client reactions.
- Avoidance of rigid or mechanically applied mindfulness instructions.

The evaluator must distinguish present-moment awareness from simple requests to concentrate or relax.

### G. Values Clarification

**Competency ID:** values_clarification

Assess whether values are explored as personally chosen qualities of action.

The evaluator should consider:

- Exploration of personally meaningful directions.
- Distinction between values and outcomes.
- Exploration of social expectations.
- Clarification of personally chosen directions.
- Sensitivity to uncertainty and ambivalence.

Do not confuse values with:

- Performance targets.
- Social expectations.
- External approval.
- Concrete outcomes.
- Obligations imposed by other people.

The evaluator must not assume that a hypothesized value in the hidden case formulation is an established value expressed by the client.

### H. Committed Action

**Competency ID:** committed_action

Assess whether proposed actions are:

- Values-consistent.
- Concrete.
- Feasible.
- Collaboratively selected.
- Sensitive to barriers and uncertainty.

The evaluator should consider:

- Connection between action and values.
- Realistic planning.
- Exploration of potential obstacles.
- Respect for client autonomy.
- Flexibility when plans need modification.

A homework assignment is not automatically evidence of committed action.

### I. Clinical Responsiveness

**Competency ID:** clinical_responsiveness

Assess whether the therapist adapts to the client's actual responses rather than rigidly following a predefined technique sequence.

The evaluator should consider:

- Responsiveness to hesitation.
- Recognition of misunderstanding.
- Adaptation to client feedback.
- Appropriate pacing.
- Willingness to revise an intervention.
- Avoidance of mechanical technique delivery.

A technically appropriate intervention may still be poorly timed or insufficiently responsive to the client.

### J. Safety and Professional Boundaries

**Competency ID:** safety_professional_boundaries

Assess:

- Appropriate safety inquiry when indicated by the available information.
- Respect for client autonomy.
- Avoidance of unsupported diagnostic claims.
- Appropriate professional boundaries.
- Recognition of limitations.
- Appropriate handling of safety-related disclosures.

The evaluator must not assume the presence or absence of risk solely from general emotional language.

The evaluator must not penalize the therapist for failing to investigate a safety issue that was neither indicated nor reasonably apparent in the available interaction.

## 4. Evidence Requirements

Every competency rating must contain the following elements.

1. Competency identifier.
2. Rating or NE.
3. Supporting transcript message identifiers.
4. Exact supporting excerpt or transcript span.
5. Explanation of the rating.
6. Limitations or missing evidence.
7. An alternative therapist response where improvement is appropriate.
8. Confidence level.

### 4.1 Evidence References

Every supporting reference must identify:

- message_id
- message role
- evidence excerpt
- start offset, when available
- end offset, when available

Evidence references must point to actual messages in the evaluated session.

The evaluator must not invent:

- Transcript excerpts.
- Message identifiers.
- Therapist statements.
- Interventions.
- Clinical observations.

### 4.2 Evidence Interpretation

The evaluator must distinguish:

- Directly observable therapist behavior.
- Patient statements.
- Interpretation of therapist behavior.
- Missing information.
- Uncertain conclusions.

A therapist's apparent intention must not be presented as an observed behavior.

### 4.3 Alternative Responses

When improvement is appropriate, the evaluator should provide a concrete alternative response that:

- Addresses the observed limitation.
- Remains consistent with ACT principles.
- Respects client autonomy.
- Is appropriate to the conversational context.
- Avoids presenting one rigid response as the only correct intervention.

An alternative response should be omitted when the competency is adequately demonstrated and no meaningful improvement is supported by evidence.

### 4.4 Confidence Level

Confidence refers to the evaluator's confidence in the evidence supporting its rating.

Allowed values:

- low
- medium
- high

Confidence is not a probability of therapist competence or a clinical certainty measure.

A low-confidence rating should include an explanation of the uncertainty.

## 5. Evaluation Procedure

The Evaluator Agent must follow the same procedure for all competency dimensions.

### Step 1 — Review the Transcript

Read the complete finalized session transcript in chronological order.

### Step 2 — Identify Relevant Opportunities

Identify opportunities in which the therapist could reasonably demonstrate the relevant competency.

### Step 3 — Identify Observable Behavior

Identify actual therapist statements and actions.

### Step 4 — Link Evidence

Connect every substantive judgment to valid transcript messages.

### Step 5 — Apply Rating Anchors

Select the most appropriate rating according to the defined anchors.

### Step 6 — Assess Missing Evidence

Use NE when the transcript does not provide enough evidence for a defensible judgment.

### Step 7 — Write Feedback

Provide concise explanations, meaningful limitations, and alternative responses where appropriate.

### Step 8 — Validate the Report

The application must validate competency identifiers, rating values, message references, evidence spans, and required fields before accepting the report.

## 6. Scoring Policy

The initial version follows a dimension-level scoring policy.

Requirements:

- Report individual competency ratings.
- Preserve NE values.
- Do not calculate a validated overall competency score.
- Do not establish pass/fail thresholds.
- Do not assign automatic weights to competencies.
- Do not compare trainees as though this were a standardized clinical examination.
- Do not reward technique counts.
- Do not infer therapist competence solely from simulated patient improvement.

Overall scoring, weighting, and comparative ranking require expert review and empirical validation.

### 6.1 Missing Evidence

Missing evidence must be reported explicitly.

The evaluator must distinguish:

- No relevant opportunity.
- Opportunity present but behavior not demonstrated.
- Ambiguous therapist behavior.
- Insufficient transcript information.

These situations must not automatically receive the same rating.

---

## 7. Relationship Between Therapist Behavior and Patient Response

The patient response provides interaction context but is not a direct measurement of therapist competence.

The evaluator must consider:

- Whether the therapist's response was clinically and contextually appropriate.
- Whether the therapist responded to the patient's actual communication.
- Whether the intervention was collaborative.
- Whether the patient response provides relevant evidence about the interaction.

The evaluator must not:

- Reward the therapist merely because the patient agrees.
- Penalize the therapist merely because the patient declines an exercise.
- Assume patient improvement proves successful intervention.
- Assume lack of immediate progress proves poor therapist performance.
- Use hidden patient state values to determine ratings.

## 8. Session-Level Evaluation Output

The expected evaluation report must be compatible with the TheraSim EvaluationReport data contract.

Each report must include:

- evaluation_id
- evaluation_scope = session
- session_id
- run_id
- rubric_id
- rubric_version
- overall_summary
- competency_results
- strengths
- improvement_areas
- missing_evidence
- uncertainty_notes
- evaluator_model
- evaluator_prompt_version
- generated_at

Each competency result must include:

- competency_id
- rating
- justification
- evidence_references
- alternative_response, when appropriate
- limitations
- uncertainty_notes

The report must reference the exact session transcript version that was evaluated.

No unsupported aggregate score may be included.

## 9. Validation Requirements

Before research deployment, this rubric requires:

- Review by qualified ACT practitioners.
- Review of competency definitions.
- Examination of item clarity.
- Content validity assessment.
- Comparison with independent human expert ratings.
- Inter-rater reliability examination.
- Evaluation of evaluator model calibration.
- Audit of unsupported evaluator claims.
- Assessment of rating consistency across different local models.

The rubric must remain versioned and traceable to its theoretical source framework and subsequent revisions.

The specific source framework and references must be documented before making claims about content validity.

---

## 10. Important Limitations

This is an experimental educational rubric.

It has not been established as a psychometrically validated measure.

It must not be used to:

- Certify therapists.
- Diagnose clients.
- Determine clinical treatment effectiveness.
- Replace clinical supervision.
- Establish professional competency thresholds.
- Make employment or licensing decisions.

Its initial use is limited to formative feedback within the TheraSim synthetic ACT simulation environment.
