# TheraSim — ACT Longitudinal Evaluator Rubric

**Rubric ID:** ACT_LONGITUDINAL_RUBRIC_V0
**Version:** 0.2
**Status:** Experimental / Not Validated
**Evaluation Scope:** Selected completed sessions in a Simulation Run
**Last Updated:** 2026-11-14

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This rubric evaluates observable therapist behavior across the explicitly selected completed sessions of a psychotherapy simulation Run.

It is designed to provide formative educational feedback about therapeutic continuity, adaptation, and consistency with Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles.

Unlike the session-level ACT rubric, this rubric focuses on how the therapist's work develops across time.

The evaluation examines:

- Continuity of therapeutic formulation.
- Integration of information disclosed in previous sessions.
- Follow-up of agreed therapeutic activities.
- Adaptation to patient responses.
- Continuity of ACT-consistent processes.
- Development and maintenance of the therapeutic relationship.
- Management of uncertainty and nonlinear change.
- Longitudinal responsiveness.
- Safety and professional boundaries across sessions.

This rubric is experimental and has not been psychometrically validated.

It is not a clinical certification instrument, diagnostic tool, or replacement for professional supervision.

### 1.1 Evaluation Scope

This rubric applies to a selected scope containing at least two completed sessions. A Run may contain any number of sessions. An interim evaluation may use a selected completed subset; a final evaluation uses all completed sessions in a formally completed Run.

The Evaluator Agent must receive:

1. The finalized transcript for every session in the selected scope.
2. Therapist-visible context available during each included session.
3. The exact longitudinal rubric version.

The sessions must be ordered chronologically and the selected scope must include at least two sessions. No fixed upper session count applies.

The evaluator must not receive hidden case formulation, undisclosed personal history, private clinical state variables, internal Patient Agent instructions, or future planned responses.

### 1.2 Relationship with Session-Level Evaluation

This rubric complements:

`ACT_RUBRIC_V0 — Version 0.2`

The two rubrics have different purposes.

**Session-Level Rubric:**

Evaluates therapist behavior within one completed session.

**Longitudinal Rubric:**

Evaluates continuity and adaptation across the selected sessions.

The longitudinal rubric must not simply calculate an average of session-level evaluations.

## 2. Scoring Anchors

Each competency is rated using the same four-level scale as the session-level ACT rubric.

### 0 — Absent or Potentially Counterproductive

The relevant longitudinal behavior is absent despite a reasonable opportunity, or the therapist repeatedly demonstrates patterns that undermine continuity or responsiveness.

### 1 — Emerging

Some continuity or adaptation is demonstrated, but it is inconsistent, superficial, or insufficiently integrated across sessions.

### 2 — Adequate

The therapist maintains reasonable continuity and adapts interventions to relevant information and interaction history.

### 3 — Strong

The therapist demonstrates consistent, flexible, collaborative, and context-sensitive therapeutic work across the selected sessions.

### NE — Not Enough Evidence

The available transcripts do not provide sufficient evidence to evaluate the competency.

NE must never be automatically converted to zero.

### 2.1 Longitudinal Rating Principles

1. Ratings must reflect observable behavior across multiple sessions.
2. The evaluator must consider chronological development.
3. The evaluator must not reward progress merely because the client appears more cooperative.
4. The evaluator must not penalize the therapist merely because the client remains distressed or avoidant.
5. Changes in therapeutic strategy must be assessed in their conversational context.
6. Consistency must not be confused with rigidly repeating the same intervention.
7. Adaptation may be evidence of therapeutic competence even when the client's responses remain uncertain.
8. A competency that was never reasonably relevant across the available sessions may receive NE.
9. The evaluator must distinguish therapist behavior from simulated patient outcomes.
10. Hidden case information must never be used as an evaluation criterion.

## 3. Longitudinal Competency Dimensions

The rubric contains nine competency dimensions.

### A. Continuity of Therapeutic Formulation

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_formulation_continuity

Assess whether the therapist maintains a coherent and evolving understanding of the client's difficulties across the selected sessions.

The evaluator should examine:

- Continuity of previously explored patterns.
- Consistency in understanding relevant triggers.
- Attention to avoidance and its consequences.
- Integration of previously disclosed information.
- Appropriate revision of earlier hypotheses.
- Avoidance of contradictory or unsupported formulations.

The evaluator must distinguish between:

- Revising a formulation in response to new evidence.
- Forgetting relevant information.
- Maintaining an unsupported interpretation despite contradictory information.

A therapist should not be penalized for changing an earlier hypothesis when the available evidence justifies the change.

The evaluator must not compare the therapist's formulation with hidden clinical information unavailable during the sessions.

### B. Integration of Client Disclosures

**Competency ID:** disclosure_integration

Assess whether the therapist appropriately uses information disclosed by the client in earlier sessions.

The evaluator should examine:

- Recognition of previously disclosed experiences.
- Appropriate reference to earlier discussions.
- Integration of relevant personal information.
- Avoidance of repeatedly asking for information already established.
- Respect for the client's disclosure boundaries.
- Sensitivity to uncertainty or discomfort following disclosure.

The evaluator must distinguish between information that was actually disclosed and information that existed only in the hidden case definition.

A therapist must not be penalized for failing to use information that was never disclosed or otherwise made available.

### C. Follow-Up and Continuity of Agreed Activities

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_homework_followup

Assess whether the therapist appropriately follows up on therapeutic activities or exercises previously agreed upon with the client.

The evaluator should examine:

- Review of previously agreed activities.
- Exploration of what the client attempted.
- Exploration of barriers to completion.
- Attention to reported outcomes.
- Nonjudgmental discussion of incomplete homework.
- Appropriate modification of future activities.
- Connection between activities and values when relevant.

The evaluator must not assume that homework completion represents therapeutic success.

Likewise, incomplete homework must not automatically be interpreted as poor therapist performance.

The evaluation should focus on how the therapist responds to the reported experience.

If no activity was agreed upon, use NE when there is insufficient evidence to assess this competency.

### D. Adaptation to Client Responses

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_adaptation

Assess whether the therapist adapts therapeutic work in response to the client's actual reactions across sessions.

The evaluator should examine:

- Responsiveness to hesitation and resistance.
- Adaptation after unsuccessful or uncomfortable interventions.
- Appropriate modification of intervention intensity.
- Avoidance of repetitive technique delivery.
- Recognition of misunderstandings.
- Adjustment to newly disclosed information.
- Ability to change direction when clinically or conversationally appropriate.

The therapist must not be expected to follow a fixed sequence of ACT techniques.

Flexible adaptation is more important than rigid completion of a predefined intervention checklist.

### E. Continuity of ACT Processes

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_act_process_continuity

Assess whether the therapist maintains an ACT-consistent approach across the selected sessions.

The evaluator should consider:

- Psychological flexibility as an overarching therapeutic direction.
- Contextual exploration of experiential avoidance.
- Acceptance and willingness where appropriate.
- Cognitive defusion when relevant.
- Present-moment awareness.
- Values clarification.
- Values-consistent committed action.
- Integration of ACT processes rather than isolated technique delivery.

The evaluator must not require every ACT process to be demonstrated in every session.

The selection of therapeutic processes must remain responsive to the client's needs and the available conversational opportunities.

Mentioning ACT terminology or completing exercises alone does not establish continuity of ACT-consistent work.

### F. Development of the Therapeutic Relationship

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_therapeutic_relationship

Assess the therapist's ability to maintain a collaborative and respectful therapeutic relationship across sessions.

The evaluator should examine:

- Respectful communication.
- Consistent empathy.
- Attention to client feedback.
- Appropriate handling of interpersonal misunderstandings.
- Collaborative decision-making.
- Respect for autonomy.
- Responsiveness to changes in engagement.
- Appropriate repair following interaction difficulties.

The evaluator must distinguish genuine responsiveness from superficial reassurance or excessive agreement.

A temporary reduction in client openness does not automatically indicate a deterioration in therapist performance.

### G. Management of Nonlinear Change and Uncertainty

**Competency ID:** nonlinear_change_responsiveness

Assess whether the therapist responds appropriately to variable, incomplete, contradictory, or nonlinear client experiences.

The evaluator should examine:

- Acceptance of fluctuating willingness.
- Appropriate response to renewed avoidance.
- Recognition of partial progress.
- Exploration of setbacks without judgment.
- Avoidance of premature conclusions.
- Revision of hypotheses when necessary.
- Tolerance of unresolved issues.
- Ability to maintain therapeutic direction despite uncertainty.

The evaluator must not expect the client to demonstrate continuous improvement.

A client may show greater openness in one session and renewed avoidance in another.

The therapist should be evaluated on their response to this pattern, not on the direction of change itself.

### H. Continuity of Values and Committed Action

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_values_action

Assess whether the therapist maintains an appropriate connection between values exploration and behavioral action across sessions.

The evaluator should examine:

- Reference to previously explored values.
- Ongoing clarification of personally meaningful directions.
- Appropriate revision of value-related hypotheses.
- Connection between values and agreed actions.
- Exploration of obstacles.
- Responsiveness to unsuccessful or incomplete actions.
- Support for flexible, client-selected behavior.

The evaluator must not treat a previously hypothesized value as an established client value unless the client has explicitly endorsed or clarified it.

Committed action must not be reduced to goal completion or task compliance.

### I. Safety and Professional Boundaries Across Sessions

**Competency ID:** longitudinal_safety_boundaries

Assess whether the therapist maintains appropriate safety awareness and professional boundaries throughout the simulation.

The evaluator should examine:

- Responsiveness to safety-related information.
- Appropriate follow-up when new concerns emerge.
- Consistency of professional boundaries.
- Avoidance of unsupported clinical conclusions.
- Appropriate acknowledgment of limitations.
- Context-sensitive safety inquiry when indicated.
- Responsible handling of sensitive disclosures.

The evaluator must not assume the presence or absence of risk based solely on general emotional language.

The absence of a safety event does not automatically establish that the therapist demonstrated strong safety assessment skills.

When the transcripts do not provide sufficient opportunity to assess this dimension, use NE.

## 4. Cross-Session Evidence Requirements

Longitudinal evaluation must use evidence from the complete simulation history.

Each competency rating must contain:

1. Competency identifier.
2. Rating or NE.
3. Evidence from one or more sessions.
4. Message identifiers.
5. Session numbers.
6. Exact excerpts or transcript spans when available.
7. Explanation of the longitudinal pattern.
8. Strengths or limitations.
9. Uncertainty information.
10. Alternative therapist responses where improvement is justified.

### 4.1 Evidence References

Each evidence reference must contain:

- session_id
- session_number
- message_id
- message_role
- evidence_excerpt
- evidence_start_offset, when available
- evidence_end_offset, when available

Evidence references must point to actual messages in the evaluated run.

At least two sessions should be referenced when the competency explicitly concerns cross-session continuity and sufficient evidence exists.

A single-session reference may be included when it provides necessary context, but it must not be presented as proof of a longitudinal pattern by itself.

### 4.2 Evidence Interpretation

The evaluator must distinguish:

- Direct therapist behavior.
- Direct patient statements.
- Patterns observed across sessions.
- Interpretation of therapist behavior.
- Missing evidence.
- Uncertain conclusions.

The evaluator must not fabricate a continuous pattern from unrelated isolated statements.

### 4.3 Chronological Integrity

The evaluator must preserve the order of sessions.

When identifying a pattern, it should clarify:

- Which sessions provide the evidence, identified by their actual session numbers.
- How the therapist responded to the earlier interaction.

A conclusion about improvement or deterioration must be supported by chronological evidence.

## 5. Longitudinal Evaluation Procedure

The Evaluator Agent must follow this procedure.

### Step 1 — Verify the Simulation Run

Confirm that at least two sessions are included, and that every included session is completed with a finalized transcript.

### Step 2 — Read the Complete History

Read every transcript in the selected scope in chronological order.

### Step 3 — Identify Cross-Session Patterns

Identify relevant patterns in formulation, therapeutic interventions, disclosures, and client responses.

### Step 4 — Identify Opportunities

Determine whether each longitudinal competency had a reasonable opportunity to be demonstrated.

### Step 5 — Extract Evidence

Select message references from the relevant sessions.

### Step 6 — Evaluate Adaptation

Assess whether the therapist responded appropriately to new information, difficulties, and changes in client engagement.

### Step 7 — Apply Rating Anchors

Assign the appropriate rating using the defined scale.

### Step 8 — Identify Missing Evidence

Use NE when the longitudinal record does not support a defensible assessment.

### Step 9 — Generate Feedback

Provide a concise description of the observed pattern and actionable recommendations.

### Step 10 — Validate the Report

The application must verify rubric identifiers, rating values, session references, message identifiers, and evidence spans.

## 7. Aggregate Scoring Policy

The initial longitudinal rubric uses competency-level ratings.

It does not define:

- An overall numerical score.
- A pass/fail decision.
- An automatic ranking of trainees.
- A validated therapist competence threshold.
- A weighted sum of session-level ratings.

The evaluator must not calculate an average of session-level ratings as a substitute for longitudinal assessment.

The primary purpose is to assess continuity and adaptation.

### 7.1 Missing Evidence

Missing evidence must be reported explicitly.

The evaluator should distinguish:

- Competency not relevant to the available simulation.
- Insufficient opportunity to demonstrate the competency.
- Ambiguous therapist behavior.
- Incomplete transcript information.
- Contradictory evidence.

These situations must not automatically receive the same rating.

---

## 8. Relationship Between Client Progress and Therapist Performance

Client progress is not the primary scoring criterion.

The evaluator must distinguish between:

1. Therapist behavior.
2. Simulated patient response.
3. Changes in structured simulation parameters.
4. Apparent short-term behavioral change.
5. Inferred long-term therapeutic outcomes.

The evaluator must not:

- Reward a therapist simply because the client appears more cooperative in a later session.
- Penalize a therapist because avoidance remains present.
- Assume that homework completion proves successful therapy.
- Assume that a client setback proves poor therapist performance.
- Use hidden state variables to generate ratings.
- Infer clinical effectiveness from simulated interactions or a short simulation history.

The evaluator should assess the therapist's ability to respond appropriately to the client's actual behavior and available information.

## 9. Feedback Requirements

Longitudinal feedback should help the trainee understand how their therapeutic work developed across sessions.

The report should include:

### 9.1 Strengths

Identify meaningful strengths supported by evidence across the simulation.

Examples:

- Maintaining a coherent functional formulation.
- Revisiting previously disclosed information appropriately.
- Adapting interventions after client hesitation.
- Maintaining collaboration despite renewed avoidance.
- Connecting values exploration to feasible action.

### 9.2 Improvement Areas

Identify recurring or important limitations supported by evidence.

Examples:

- Failure to revisit an agreed activity.
- Repeated interventions without evaluating the client's response.
- Inconsistent attention to previously discussed patterns.
- Premature conclusions about client progress.
- Insufficient integration of values and committed action.

### 9.3 Alternative Responses

Where improvement is warranted, provide a concrete alternative therapist response.

Recommendations must:

- Refer to an actual interaction.
- Be consistent with ACT principles.
- Respect client autonomy.
- Address the specific limitation.
- Avoid presenting one rigid intervention sequence as mandatory.

### 9.4 Uncertainty Notes

The evaluator must explicitly report when the available multi-session record is insufficient to support a conclusion.

---

## 10. Validation Requirements

This rubric must undergo further review before any claims of validity are made.

Required validation activities include:

- Review by qualified ACT practitioners.
- Expert assessment of longitudinal competencies.
- Examination of item clarity and relevance.
- Independent evaluation of sample transcripts.
- Inter-rater reliability assessment.
- Comparison between AI-generated and human ratings.
- Analysis of false-positive and false-negative evaluations.
- Examination of evaluator consistency across different local models.
- Review of Persian-language interpretations.

Longitudinal validity cannot be established through agreement on a single synthetic case.

Future studies should include a larger and more diverse collection of synthetic cases and transcripts.

---

## 11. Important Limitations

This is an experimental formative evaluation rubric.

It has not been validated as a psychometric instrument.

It must not be used to:

- Certify therapists.
- Determine professional competence for licensing.
- Diagnose clients.
- Claim successful treatment outcomes.
- Replace human supervision.
- Make employment or credentialing decisions.

Its use is limited to formative educational feedback within ACT simulation Runs; it does not establish clinical effectiveness or validated therapist competence.

The quality of the rubric and the validity of the Evaluator Agent must be studied separately.
