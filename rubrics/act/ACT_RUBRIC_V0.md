# TheraSim - ACT Evaluator Rubric V0

Version: 0.1
Status: Experimental / Not Validated

IMPORTANT:
This is a prototype educational rubric.
It is not a validated competency instrument,
clinical certification system, or replacement
for professional supervision.

## 1. Scoring Anchors

0 - Absent or potentially counterproductive:
The relevant behavior is absent or the therapist's
response may undermine the therapeutic process.

1 - Emerging:
The skill is partially demonstrated but is
inconsistent, poorly timed, or insufficiently
collaborative.

2 - Adequate:
The skill is demonstrated in a relevant and
reasonably context-sensitive manner.

3 - Strong:
The skill is applied flexibly, collaboratively,
and with clear attention to the client's context.

NE - Not Enough Evidence:
The transcript does not provide enough evidence
to make a defensible judgment.

NE must not be converted into a zero.

## 2. Evaluation Dimensions

### A. Therapeutic Relationship

Assess:

- Empathy
- Validation
- Collaboration
- Respect for client autonomy
- Sensitivity to hesitation

### B. Functional Assessment

Assess:

- Identification of triggers
- Exploration of private experiences
- Identification of avoidance behavior
- Examination of short-term consequences
- Examination of longer-term consequences

### C. Psychological Workability

Assess whether the therapist explores
whether the client's current strategies
move the client toward or away from
meaningful life activities.

### D. Acceptance and Willingness

Assess whether the therapist appropriately
explores openness to difficult experiences
instead of focusing exclusively on their elimination.

### E. Cognitive Defusion

Assess whether the therapist helps the client
change their relationship with thoughts rather
than relying exclusively on disputing thought content.

### F. Present-Moment Awareness

Assess appropriate attention to ongoing
thoughts, feelings, sensations, and experiences.

### G. Values Clarification

Assess whether values are explored as
personally chosen qualities of action.

Do not confuse values with outcomes,
social expectations, or performance targets.

### H. Committed Action

Assess whether actions are:

- Values-consistent
- Concrete
- Feasible
- Collaboratively selected
- Sensitive to barriers and uncertainty

### I. Clinical Responsiveness

Assess whether the therapist adapts to
the client's actual responses rather than
rigidly following a predefined technique sequence.

### J. Longitudinal Continuity

Assess whether the therapist:

- Remembers previous disclosures
- Reviews agreed exercises
- Maintains a coherent formulation
- Revises hypotheses when new evidence emerges

### K. Safety and Professional Boundaries

Assess appropriate safety inquiry,
respect for autonomy, avoidance of unsupported
diagnostic claims, and appropriate professional
boundaries.

## 3. Evidence Requirements

Every rating must contain:

1. Dimension name.
2. Score or NE.
3. Exact transcript excerpt.
4. Explanation of the rating.
5. An alternative response where improvement
   is appropriate.
6. Confidence level.

The evaluator must distinguish observed behavior
from inferred therapist intentions.

The evaluator must never penalize the therapist
for not discovering information that the patient
had not disclosed and the therapist had no
reasonable opportunity to elicit.

## 4. Longitudinal Evaluation

Evaluate separately:

- Performance in each session.
- Continuity across sessions.
- Changes in therapist behavior.
- Consistency between formulation and interventions.
- Response to client feedback.

Do not infer therapist competence solely from
simulated patient improvement.

Do not reward technique counts.

## 5. Scoring Policy

Initial version:

- Report dimension-level ratings.
- Preserve NE values.
- Do not calculate a validated competency score.
- Do not establish pass/fail thresholds.
- Do not compare users as if this were a
  standardized clinical examination.

Overall scoring and weighting require
expert review and empirical validation.

## 6. Validation Requirements

Before research deployment:

- Review items with ACT practitioners.
- Establish content validity.
- Test rating clarity.
- Compare evaluator judgments with independent
  human expert ratings.
- Examine inter-rater reliability.
- Evaluate model calibration.
- Audit unsupported evaluator claims.

The rubric must remain versioned and traceable
to its source framework and subsequent revisions.
